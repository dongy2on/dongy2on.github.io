Structural Stability Analysis

Evaluation and Improvement of Structural Stability under Earthquake using Response Spectrum

2024. 66th Gyeongnam Science Exhibition Entry  ·  창과방패 (Dongyeon Kwon, Sihyeon Kim, Yeseo Ahn)
1. Motivation and Purpose

As the frequency of earthquakes in Korea has gradually increased since the 2000s, the scale of damage caused by building collapses has also grown. This study was initiated to devise a seismic design method capable of effectively distributing forces, inspired by the Truss structure, in order to minimize earthquake damage.

The primary objective is to evaluate the stability of three commonly used seismic design structures using the response spectrum, compare them with a directly designed truss structure model, and derive the safest seismic design strategy.

2. Research Methods & Tools
MIDAS NFX (3D Modeling) Response Spectrum Earthquake Simulator Truss Design

A. Building Design & Model Production
A total of three models were produced: a 'Basic Structure' with a simple frame, a commonly used 'Double Warren Truss Structure', and a newly designed 'Seismic Structure' configured in a triangular shape that accounts for both vertical and horizontal loads.

B. Earthquake Simulation Experiment
By visually comparing the movement of each building model using an earthquake simulator, it was confirmed that the directly designed structure effectively suppressed displacement and maintained stability far better than the existing structures.

C. 3D Modeling & Response Spectrum Analysis
For a precise quantitative evaluation, the buildings were 3D modeled using MIDAS NFX software. After inputting vertical and horizontal seismic loads, displacement and maximum stress based on natural frequency were calculated through response spectrum analysis.

4. Results & Expected Effects

A. Comparison of Analysis Results
After analyzing the response spectrum of each structure across 150 modes, the average error value of the newly designed structure (0.000302) was found to be significantly lower than that of the basic structure (0.00332) and the Double Warren Truss structure (0.000438). This clearly indicates that the proposed structure experiences the least displacement and deformation during a seismic event.

B. Expected Effects
This study established a reliable foundation for numerically evaluating the stability of diverse design structures. Applying the proposed optimal structural design to actual architectural projects is expected to drastically reduce the risk of building collapse and ensure public safety during earthquakes.
